Drawing basics: lines, circles, shapes and cubes: every artist's warm-up

These exercises aren't flashy, but they make your hand steady. Do a few minutes before you draw: your lines will be cleaner and your shapes more accurate.

  • Beginner
  • 10 min practice
  • 2 min read
  • Updated Nov 11, 2026

Key takeaways

  • A quick, slightly curved line beats a slow, shaky one.
  • No points at the ends of an ellipse: they stay rounded.
  • Draw big enough: a stamp-sized shape teaches you nothing.
  • Light hidden edges help you see whether the cube holds together.

Confident lines

  1. Step 1Move from the shoulder

    For long lines, lock your wrist and move your whole arm. The line gets straighter and more even.

  2. Step 2Ghost the stroke

    Place two points, move back and forth above the paper without touching it, then draw in one go.

  3. Step 3Light first

    Start with light strokes, then strengthen only the useful lines.

Tip

A quick, slightly curved line beats a slow, shaky one.

Circles and ellipses

  1. Step 1Go around in one gesture

    Go around the shape in one fluid motion, two or three times on top, rather than in small hesitant strokes.

  2. Step 2An ellipse is a tilted circle

    The more you see the circle from the side, the flatter the ellipse. Its two halves always stay symmetrical.

  3. Step 3Draw its axis

    A line through the middle of the ellipse, along its length, helps you keep it straight and even.

Tip

No points at the ends of an ellipse: they stay rounded.

Simple shapes

  1. Step 1Corners first

    Place the four corners of a square as light dots, then join them. Easier than drawing side by side.

  2. Step 2Vary the sizes

    Switch between small and big shapes: big ones train your arm, small ones your accuracy.

  3. Step 3Check upside down

    Turn your page around: tilted sides and wrong angles jump out.

Tip

Draw big enough: a stamp-sized shape teaches you nothing.

From square to cube

  1. Step 1One face, then depth

    Draw the front face, then three parallel edges leaving its corners in the same direction.

  2. Step 2Close the volume

    Join the ends of the edges: the back face appears. Draw the hidden edges lightly too.

  3. Step 3Tilt it

    Do it again with a tilted cube: no edge is horizontal anymore, but parallel edges stay parallel.

Tip

Light hidden edges help you see whether the cube holds together.

The coach's tips

  • Before each session, 1 minute of lines and ellipses is enough to loosen your hand.
  • Hold your pencil further from the tip for big strokes, closer for details.
  • Don't erase every mistake right away: spot what doesn't work and correct it on top.
  • Rotate your page so you always draw in the direction that's most comfortable for your hand.
  • Work fast rather than perfect: speed makes lines straighter.
