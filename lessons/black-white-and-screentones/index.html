Black, white and screentones: solid blacks, screentones and black-and-white readability

Manga is read in black and white: without color, solid blacks and screentones separate the shapes, create the shadows and set the mood. Well balanced, they give a page its impact.

  • Intermediate
  • 15 min practice
  • 2 min read
  • Updated Nov 11, 2026

Key takeaways

  • A page without any black looks bland; a page with too much becomes unreadable. Aim for a balance of white, gray and black.
  • Keep the same mood from panel to panel within a scene: a change of tone tells the reader the moment has changed.
  • Squint at your page: if everything turns gray, add blacks.
  • Test your blacks on a copy of the drawing before touching the original.

Solid blacks

  1. Step 1Choose what is black

    Dark hair, dark clothes, deep shadows: decide at the pencil stage which areas will be black.

  2. Step 2Balance the page

    Spread the blacks over the whole page rather than bunching them in a corner. The eye travels from one to the next.

  3. Step 3Black makes things stand out

    A light character in front of a black background, or the reverse, reads instantly. Avoid gray on gray.

  4. Step 4Keep highlights

    Leave a few white lines in big blacks, on hair or a leather garment: the black keeps its volume.

Tip

A page without any black looks bland; a page with too much becomes unreadable. Aim for a balance of white, gray and black.

Screentones

  • A screentone is a pattern of dots that makes a gray. The bigger or tighter the dots, the darker the gray.
  • Use few different grays: two or three values are enough for a whole page.
  • A light tone for skin in shadow or colored clothes, a darker one for shadows and backgrounds.
  • A gradient tone for a sky, falling light or an emotional background.
  • On a tablet, drawing apps have ready-made tones; on paper, hatching does the same job.

Mood backgrounds

  1. Step 1Focus lines

    Lines converging on the character: surprise, revelation, shock.

  2. Step 2Flowers and bubbles

    A background of flowers, bubbles or sparkles: a romantic moment or a dazzling character.

  3. Step 3Black and vertical lines

    A black background, or one striped with vertical lines: despair, threat, unease.

Moods: night, rain and memories

Tones aren't only for shading: they change the time and place of a scene without a word.

  1. Step 1Night

    A dark tone over the whole sky and the backgrounds, the character left light where a light falls on them. Bold solid blacks for the shadows.

  2. Step 2Rain

    Thin white lines scratched diagonally over a gray tone, all in the same direction. Add splashes on the ground.

  3. Step 3A memory

    Panels with blurred or rounded edges, a black background around the panels, or a soft light tone over the whole scene.

  4. Step 4Tension

    Backgrounds getting darker from one panel to the next: the page darkens with the situation.

Tip

Keep the same mood from panel to panel within a scene: a change of tone tells the reader the moment has changed.

The coach's tips

  • Squint at your page: if everything turns gray, add blacks.
  • Test your blacks on a copy of the drawing before touching the original.
  • Don't tone everything: the white areas matter as much as the gray ones.
  • Look at a manga page from a distance: you'll see its big black shapes. That's its structure.
  • In the app, trace a character, then choose their black areas: hair, clothes, shadows.

Practice right now

Once the lesson is read, draw this exercise: it takes about 15 minutes.

Practice exercise

Take one of your drawings and make three versions of it: line only, with solid blacks, then with blacks and a single gray in hatching or tone.
