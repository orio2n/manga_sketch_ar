How to draw an anime face, step by step

To draw an anime face, build the head first: a sphere for the skull, a flat plane on its side, a vertical axis down the face, an eye line across it and a jaw down to the chin. Then place the features on those guides: the eyes on the eye line, the nose and mouth on the center line, the ears between the eye line and the bottom of the nose, and the hair around the skull.

  • Beginner
  • 40 min practice

  • Updated Oct 24, 2026

You'll need

  • HB pencil
  • 2B pencil
  • Eraser
  • Printer paper or a sketchbook

Key takeaways

  • Build the head as a volume before drawing any feature: skull, side plane, vertical axis, eye line, jaw.
  • The eyes sit on a line about halfway down the head; the nose and mouth sit on the center line below them.
  • The ears fit between the eye line and the bottom of the nose, on the side of the skull.
  • The jaw, the eyes and the hairstyle make the character: change them, not the construction.

What makes a face look anime?

An anime face is a real face simplified. The eyes get bigger and more detailed, while the nose shrinks to a small wedge or a single mark and the mouth to a short line. The cheeks and the jaw become one smooth curve that ends in a soft or pointed chin.

Because so much is left out, every line you keep has to be in the right place. That is why anime artists build the head first and only then add the features: a small eye drawn on the wrong line is noticed right away, while a perfect eye on a misbuilt head still looks wrong.

  • Big eyes on a line near the middle of the head, about one eye's width apart.
  • A small nose, often just the tip or a shadow on one side.
  • A small mouth, closer to the nose than to the chin.
  • A simple jaw that goes from the ears to the chin in one or two curves.
  • Hair drawn as big shapes that sit on top of the skull, not glued to it.

Anime face proportions

These proportions are a starting point, not a law. Learn them on a front view and a profile, then bend them to fit your style: younger characters get lower eyes and a shorter chin, adults a longer face and a stronger jaw.

  • Eyes: on a horizontal line about halfway between the top of the skull and the chin, with one eye's width between them.
  • Nose: its tip about halfway between the eye line and the chin, on the center line.
  • Mouth: roughly a third of the way from the nose to the chin.
  • Ears: on the side of the head, from the eye line down to the bottom of the nose.
  • Neck: it starts under the ears; draw it narrower for a slender character and wider for a strong one.

How to draw an anime face step by step

This is the head method of week 4 of the SketchAI program, building the head in five steps, followed by the features. The construction is shown in a three-quarter view, the most useful angle to learn, but it works exactly the same from the front.

  1. Step 1Draw the skull

    Start with a sphere or an egg shape, wider at the top. Draw it lightly and in one or two strokes: this is the volume of the skull, not the outline of the face.

  2. Step 2Flatten the side plane

    Imagine the sphere has been sliced flat on the side. Draw that flat area as an oval or a rounded shape on the side of the skull: it shows where the temple is and where the ear will go.

  3. Step 3Draw the vertical axis

    Draw a line down the middle of the face, from the top of the skull to where the chin will be. When the head turns, this line moves toward the far side and curves around the volume.

  4. Step 4Draw the eye line

    Cross the axis with a horizontal line about halfway down the head. It wraps around the sphere like a ribbon: on a turned head, it curves too. The eyes will sit on it.

  5. Step 5Draw the jaw and the chin

    Extend the vertical axis below the sphere to mark the chin, then connect the sides of the skull to it with two simple lines. Straight, angular lines give a strong jaw; soft curves give a younger or gentler face.

  6. Step 6Place the eyes, nose and mouth

    Draw the eyes on the eye line, one eye's width apart, following the anime eyes tutorial. Mark the tip of the nose on the axis about halfway down to the chin, and a short mouth line a little below it. On a turned head, the far eye gets narrower.

  7. Step 7Add the ears, eyebrows and hairline

    Draw the ears on the side plane, between the eye line and the bottom of the nose. Add the eyebrows above the eyes and a hairline across the forehead. These three details anchor the face on the skull.

  8. Step 8Draw the hair and clean up

    Draw the hair as a few big locks that start from the hairline and rise above the skull, then add smaller strands. Erase the construction lines and go over the final lines with a darker pencil, pressing harder on the outer contour and the eyes.

How to draw male and female anime faces

The construction stays the same; the differences live in a few shapes. Many styles give male characters a longer face, a wider and more angular jaw, narrower eyes, thicker eyebrows and a wider neck.

Female characters often get a rounder face, a smaller and softer chin, bigger eyes with longer lashes, thin eyebrows and a slender neck. Age matters as much as gender: children have the roundest faces and the lowest eyes, and older characters get longer faces and a few lines on the cheeks.

How to draw the face from the side and at an angle

Keep the same five shapes for every angle. In a three-quarter view, the vertical axis moves toward the far side and the far eye narrows. In profile, the axis becomes the outline of the face: the brow, the nose, the mouth and the chin all sit on it, and the ear is drawn on the side plane, near the middle of the head.

The best practice is to draw one character from several angles in a row, checking that the eyes, the nose and the ears stay on the same guides each time. The head from any angle tutorial goes further with views from above and below.

Common mistakes when drawing anime faces

  • Starting with the eyes: without a head built first, the features end up crooked or at the wrong height. Draw the five shapes first.
  • A flat face: the eye line and the axis drawn as straight lines on a turned head. Let them curve around the volume.
  • Ears in the wrong place: too high, too low or too far forward. Keep them between the eye line and the bottom of the nose.
  • A mouth too low: anime mouths sit closer to the nose than to the chin.
  • Hair glued to the skull: hair has volume, so its outline sits above the top of the skull.

Frequently asked questions

How do you draw an anime face for beginners?

Build the head first with five shapes: a sphere for the skull, the side plane, a vertical axis, an eye line and the jaw. Then place the eyes on the eye line, the nose and mouth on the axis, and the ears on the side plane.

Where do the eyes go on an anime face?

On a horizontal line about halfway down the head, with about one eye's width between them. Younger characters often have the eyes a little lower.

What are the proportions of an anime face?

As a starting point: the eyes halfway down the head, the tip of the nose halfway between the eyes and the chin, the mouth a third of the way from the nose to the chin, and the ears between the eye line and the bottom of the nose.

How do you draw an anime face from the side?

Use the same construction: in profile, the vertical axis becomes the outline of the face, with the brow, nose, mouth and chin on it, and the ear sits on the side plane near the middle of the head.

Why does my anime face look flat?

Usually because the guidelines are drawn as straight lines on a turned head. Draw the axis and the eye line as curves that wrap around the skull, and the face will turn with them.
