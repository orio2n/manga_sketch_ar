How to draw dynamic anime poses

To draw a dynamic anime pose, start with a curved line of action that sums up the movement, then build the body along it: tilt the shoulders and the hips in opposite directions, use simple volumes for the torso and the limbs, and check that the weight sits over the foot on the ground. Add the details only once the gesture reads from far away.

  • Intermediate
  • 45 min practice
  • 6 min read
  • Updated Oct 24, 2026

You'll need

  • HB pencil
  • 2B pencil
  • Eraser
  • Printer paper or a sketchbook
  • Photos of poses for reference

Key takeaways

  • Start every pose with a line of action: one curve that sums up the movement.
  • Tilt the shoulders and the hips in different directions: they are rarely both horizontal.
  • Build the body as a mannequin along the curve, then check the balance over the supporting foot.
  • Draw the gesture fast: a loose 30-second pose has more life than a careful, stiff one.

Why poses look stiff

A stiff pose usually has the same problems: a straight spine, shoulders and hips perfectly level, the weight spread evenly on both legs and the arms mirroring each other. Real bodies are almost never symmetrical, and manga exaggerates that even more.

Dynamic poses come from a few tools you can apply to any character. Week 9 of the SketchAI program gathers them as four: the line of action, opposing axes, foreshortening and balance. The steps below use all four.

How to draw an anime pose step by step

Have a reference at hand, a photo or yourself in a mirror, and draw lightly. Each step should take seconds, not minutes: you can always correct a construction, but you cannot add life to a stiff drawing.

  1. Step 1Find the movement

    Decide what the character is doing and where the energy goes: a jump goes up and forward, a punch goes toward the target, a slouch sinks down. Do a few quick sketches of 30 to 60 seconds to try the idea before committing.

  2. Step 2Draw the line of action

    Draw one long curve that sums up the movement, from the head down to the supporting foot. A C-curve or an S-curve works for most poses; avoid a straight vertical line, which freezes the body.

  3. Step 3Place the big volumes along the curve

    Draw the head, the rib cage and the pelvis along the line of action, following its curve. Keep them simple: an oval, an egg shape and a box are enough to see whether the pose works.

  4. Step 4Tilt the shoulders and the hips

    Draw a line through the shoulders and another through the hips, and tilt them in opposite directions. When the weight rests on one leg, that hip rises and the shoulder above it drops: the pose relaxes at once.

  5. Step 5Add the limbs with cylinders

    Place spheres at the joints and connect them with cylinders. Bend the elbows and knees in the direction of the movement, and keep the arms and legs asymmetrical: one forward, one back.

  6. Step 6Check the balance

    Find the point where the body touches the ground and check that the weight sits above it. A standing pose needs the head roughly above the supporting foot; a pose that is off balance should clearly show that the character is jumping, falling or running.

  7. Step 7Foreshorten what comes toward you

    An arm or a leg pointing at the viewer looks shorter and its end looks bigger. Build it with overlapping cylinders and ellipses instead of drawing it at its full length.

  8. Step 8Draw the character over the construction

    Draw the body, the clothes and the face over the mannequin, keeping the line of action visible in the final lines. Erase the construction, then darken the outline on the side of the body that carries the weight.

Anime poses to practice

Pick one family of poses per session and draw it ten times with small variations. Each one trains a different part of the body.

  • The relaxed standing pose: weight on one leg, opposing shoulders and hips, one hand in a pocket.
  • Walking and running: the arm opposite the forward leg swings forward, and the torso leans into the movement.
  • Jumping: one long curve from the hand to the feet, the knees tucked or the legs stretched.
  • The fighting stance: feet wide apart, knees bent, torso turned, one fist forward.
  • Sitting and crouching: the pelvis box tilted and the legs folded, a great test for foreshortening.
  • Looking back: the head and the shoulders turned one way, the hips the other.

How to draw action and fighting poses

Action poses push every tool further. Use diagonal lines of action, stretch the body along them, and exaggerate the distance between the front fist or foot and the back one. A punch or a kick toward the viewer is the perfect place for foreshortening: the fist can be as big as the head.

Keep the silhouette readable: if you filled the pose with black, you should still understand the action. When limbs overlap the torso and the shape becomes a blob, turn the character or move an arm away from the body. The foreshortening tutorial covers punches and kicks in depth.

Tip

Act the pose out yourself in front of a mirror, or take a photo of a friend. Feeling where your weight goes is the fastest way to understand a pose.

How to use references for poses

References are not cheating: they show you how a body really bends. Look at the reference for a minute, find its line of action, its shoulder and hip lines and the supporting foot, then draw only those. Copying every outline would teach you less.

Alternate timed gestures from photos with poses from imagination. After a few weeks, the poses you invent start to borrow the life of the ones you studied. Our guide on drawing from reference gives a full routine.

Common mistakes when drawing anime poses

  • A straight spine: the line of action is missing, so the body looks frozen. Start with the curve.
  • Level shoulders and hips: tilt them in opposite directions when the weight is on one leg.
  • Symmetrical arms and legs: vary the angles and the depth of each limb.
  • A character that tips over: the weight is not above the supporting foot. Check the balance before adding details.
  • Details too early: drawing the face and clothes on a pose that does not work. Fix the construction first.

Frequently asked questions

How do you draw anime poses for beginners?

Start with a line of action, place the head, rib cage and pelvis along it, tilt the shoulders and hips in opposite directions, add the limbs as cylinders and check the balance. Draw the details last.

What is a line of action in drawing?

It is one curve that sums up the movement of the whole body, usually from the head to the supporting foot. Building the body along it keeps the pose from being straight and stiff.

How do you make a pose look dynamic?

Curve the line of action, tilt the shoulders and hips differently, make the limbs asymmetrical and use foreshortening for anything that comes toward the viewer.

How do you draw anime fighting poses?

Use a wide stance with bent knees, a torso turned away from the front fist, a diagonal line of action and foreshortening on the punch or kick. Keep the silhouette readable.

Should I use references to draw poses?

Yes. Study the line of action, the tilt of the shoulders and hips and the balance in a reference, then draw the pose from those, instead of copying every outline.
