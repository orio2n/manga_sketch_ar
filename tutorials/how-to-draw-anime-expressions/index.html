How to draw anime expressions and emotions

To draw anime expressions, keep the same head and change four things together: the eyebrows, the eyelids, the pupils and the mouth. Raised brows and wide eyes read as surprise, brows pulled down toward the nose read as anger, and raised inner brows with a wobbly mouth read as sadness. Manga then adds symbols like blush lines, sweat drops and tears.

Key takeaways

  • Change the eyebrows, eyelids, pupils and mouth together: the mouth alone is never enough.
  • Keep the construction of the head: an expression moves the features, not the skull.
  • The eyebrows read first, so their angle carries most of the emotion.
  • Manga symbols such as blush lines, sweat drops, tears and veins push an emotion further.

How anime expressions work

An expression is a handful of features moving at the same time. Beginners often change only the mouth and wonder why the face still looks neutral: in real faces and in manga, the eyebrows and the eyelids move first and say the most.

Week 5 of the SketchAI program puts it in one tip: for expressions, work the eyebrows, eyelids, cheeks and mouth together, instead of only changing the mouth. Think of them as five levers you can push in different directions.

  • Eyebrows: raised, lowered, pulled together, or raised at the inner ends.
  • Eyelids: wide open, relaxed, narrowed, or closed into arcs.
  • Pupils: normal, tiny with white all around, or hidden by the lids.
  • Mouth: a line, a smile, an open shape, teeth, a wobbly curve.
  • Cheeks and symbols: blush lines, sweat drops, tears, a vein on the forehead.

How to draw an anime expression step by step

Choose one emotion before you start and look at your own face in a mirror while you make it. Then build the expression in this order, from the parts that read first to the details.

  1. Step 1Build the head

    Draw the head with its vertical axis and its eye line, exactly as for a neutral face. The construction keeps the features in place, so the character stays recognizable whatever the emotion.

  2. Step 2Set the eyebrows

    Draw the eyebrows before the eyes. Lowered and pulled toward the nose for anger, raised high for surprise, raised at the inner ends for sadness or worry, relaxed and curved for happiness.

  3. Step 3Shape the eyelids and the pupils

    Open the eyes wide for surprise or fear, narrow them for anger or suspicion, close them into upward arcs for laughter. Shrink the pupils for shock, and let the upper lid cover part of the iris for sadness, boredom or menace.

  4. Step 4Draw the mouth

    Match the mouth to the eyes: a wide open smile with laughing eyes, gritted teeth with angry brows, a wobbly downturned line with sad eyes, a small round opening with a surprised look. Keep it on the center line, closer to the nose than to the chin.

  5. Step 5Add the cheeks and the manga symbols

    Add short diagonal lines on the cheeks for a blush, a drop of sweat at the temple for nerves, tears along the lower lids for sadness. Use one or two symbols, not all of them.

  6. Step 6Check the expression from a distance

    Step back or look at the drawing in a mirror. If the emotion does not read at a glance, push the eyebrows and the eyelids further before adding any detail.

Six basic anime expressions

Learn these six first. Almost every other expression is a mix of two of them, like a nervous smile (happy and embarrassed) or a cold glare (angry and determined).

  • Happy: eyes closed into arcs or wide and bright, relaxed raised brows, an open smile, a light blush.
  • Angry: brows pulled down toward the nose with a few wrinkles between them, narrowed eyes, gritted teeth or a tight mouth.
  • Sad: inner ends of the brows raised, drooping lids, tears along the lower lids, a wobbly downturned mouth.
  • Surprised: brows raised high, wide eyes with tiny pupils, a small round open mouth.
  • Embarrassed: eyes glancing aside, blush lines on the cheeks, a drop of sweat, a small frown.
  • Determined: low and straight brows, slightly narrowed eyes, a firm closed mouth.

Manga symbols for emotions

Manga has a visual vocabulary that readers understand instantly. Add these symbols on top of a clear expression; they reinforce it but cannot replace it.

  • Blush lines: short diagonal hatching on the cheeks, for embarrassment, affection or effort.
  • Sweat drop: a single big drop at the temple or the back of the head, for nervousness or awkwardness.
  • Cross-shaped vein: a small cross of curved lines on the forehead, for irritation.
  • Tears: drops along the lower lid, or streams down the cheeks for comic crying.
  • Shadow over the eyes: hatching across the top of the face, for gloom or menace.
  • Simplified eyes: dots, spirals or crosses, for comedy, dizziness or shock.

Keep the character recognizable

An expression should change the face, not the character. Keep the shape of the skull, the jaw, the hairstyle and the eye shape, and only move the eyebrows, lids, pupils and mouth. Drawing the same character with four emotions side by side is the best test.

Exaggeration depends on the tone of your story. A comedy can squash the whole face into a scream; a quiet drama may only lower the eyelids a little. Decide how far to push before you draw. The anime face tutorial explains the construction that keeps everything consistent.

Common mistakes when drawing anime expressions

  • Only the mouth changes: the eyes and brows stay neutral, so the face reads as blank. Move the brows first.
  • Mixed signals: angry brows with a happy mouth read as confusing unless you want a sly grin. Make every feature agree.
  • Features that drift: the eyes and mouth leave their guidelines and the character looks different. Keep the construction.
  • Too many symbols: blush, sweat, tears and veins all at once. One or two are enough.
  • Timid expressions: a slight change that nobody notices. Push it, then pull back if it is too much.

Frequently asked questions

How do you draw anime expressions for beginners?

Build the head first, then change the eyebrows, the eyelids, the pupils and the mouth together for the emotion you want, and add a manga symbol like a blush or a sweat drop if it helps.

How do you draw an angry anime face?

Pull the eyebrows down toward the nose with a few wrinkles between them, narrow the eyes, shrink the pupils a little and draw gritted teeth or a tight mouth.

How do you draw a crying anime face?

Raise the inner ends of the eyebrows, let the eyelids droop, add tears along the lower lids or down the cheeks, and draw a wobbly, downturned mouth.

How do you draw a surprised anime face?

Raise the eyebrows high, open the eyes wide with tiny pupils and white all around, and draw a small round open mouth.

What does the sweat drop mean in anime?

A single big drop at the temple or the back of the head shows nervousness, embarrassment or awkwardness, often in a comic moment.
