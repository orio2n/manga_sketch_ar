7 drawing exercises for beginners who want to draw anime

The best drawing exercises for beginners train the hand and the sense of volume at the same time: straight lines, circles and ellipses, boxes in perspective, cylinders and spheres, quick mannequins, gesture drawings and simplified heads. Do a few of them every day for 10 to 20 minutes, with a timer, and they will make every anime character you draw easier.

  • 5 min read
  • Updated Oct 24, 2026

Key takeaways

  • Start with line control: straight lines, circles and ellipses, each in one stroke.
  • Then train volume: boxes, cylinders and spheres that turn in space.
  • Move on to figures: quick mannequins and 30-second gestures, then simplified heads.
  • Short, daily and timed sessions work better than long, rare ones.

Why drawing exercises work

Drawing a character mixes several skills at once: controlling your lines, seeing in 3D, knowing proportions and catching a movement. When a drawing goes wrong, it is hard to tell which skill failed. Exercises isolate one skill at a time, so you can repeat it until it becomes automatic.

They are also short. Each exercise below takes 5 to 15 minutes, and a few of them together make a complete session. Draw on cheap printer paper, so you never hesitate to fill a page.

1. Straight lines

Confident lines are the base of clean anime lineart. Draw straight lines of different lengths in every direction, each in a single stroke, moving from the shoulder rather than the wrist for the long ones.

To aim, place two dots and hover your pencil above the paper between them a few times before drawing: this is called ghosting. Then draw the line quickly; speed makes it straighter than care.

Practice exercise

Fill a page with straight lines in 5 minutes: horizontal, vertical and diagonal, short and long, then lines connecting pairs of dots.

2. Circles and ellipses

Heads, eyes, joints and the ends of every cylinder are circles and ellipses. Draw them in one fluid gesture, going around the shape two or three times lightly before committing, rather than in small hesitant strokes.

Vary the size and the width of the ellipses, from almost circles to thin ovals, and tilt them in different directions. Draw a line through the long axis of each ellipse to check that it is symmetrical.

Practice exercise

Draw 30 circles and 20 ellipses without a compass in 10 minutes, then circle the five best ones and try to beat them.

3. Boxes in perspective

The box is the most useful shape in drawing: it teaches you how a volume turns in space, and the torso, the pelvis, the hands and even the head can be built from boxes. Draw one face, add the edges that show depth in a consistent direction, then close the volume.

Draw the hidden edges lightly, as if the box were made of glass, and extend the edges to check that they converge. The box in perspective tutorial explains each step.

Practice exercise

Draw 15 boxes in 15 minutes, each turned a different way: seen from above, from below and from the side.

4. Cylinders and spheres

Cylinders become arms, legs, necks and fingers, and spheres become heads and joints. Draw cylinders standing, lying and tilted, with an ellipse at each end, and spheres with lines that wrap around them like the lines on a ball.

Then put a sphere inside a box: it is a great way to check that both are drawn in the same perspective.

Practice exercise

Draw 5 cylinders from different angles and 5 spheres with their axes in 10 minutes.

5. Quick mannequins

A mannequin is a body reduced to simple volumes: an oval for the head, an egg for the rib cage, a box for the pelvis, cylinders for the limbs and spheres for the joints. Drawing many of them quickly teaches proportions and poses faster than any detailed drawing.

Do not add any clothes or details. Change the pose every time: walking, sitting, crouching, reaching. The anime body tutorial shows how to build one step by step.

Practice exercise

Draw 10 mannequins in 15 minutes, with the arms and legs in a different pose each time.

6. Gesture drawing

Gesture drawing is about the movement, not the details. Look at a photo of a person in action and capture the whole pose in 30 to 60 seconds, starting with one curve for the line of action, then the head, the torso and the limbs as simple lines.

The time limit is the point: it forces you to see the big movement instead of the small parts. Your gestures will look messy, and that is fine.

Practice exercise

Set a timer and do 10 gestures of 30 to 60 seconds from photos. Then turn your two favorites into mannequins.

7. Simplified heads

Before drawing faces, draw heads as volumes: a sphere with a vertical axis and an eye line that wrap around it, a flat plane on the side and a jaw down to the chin. Turn them in every direction.

This exercise makes faces at an angle much easier later. The head from any angle tutorial goes through the full construction.

Practice exercise

Draw 10 spheres with their axes, then turn 3 to 5 of them into simplified heads with a side plane and a jaw, in 10 minutes.

How to turn these exercises into a daily plan

You do not need all seven every day. Build a 20-minute session around one main topic and keep a few short exercises around it, so the basics stay sharp while you learn something new. The SketchAI program is built this way, and you can follow the same plan in any sketchbook.

  1. Warm-up, 1 minute: lines, circles and ellipses.
  2. Volumes, 4 minutes: boxes and cylinders from different angles.
  3. Main exercise, 11 minutes: the topic of the week, like mannequins, heads or gestures.
  4. Reference study, 4 minutes: a quick drawing from a photo or a manga panel.
Common mistake

Erasing every line that is not perfect. Exercises are meant to be messy: draw the next line instead, and look at the page as a whole when you are done.

Frequently asked questions

What should a beginner practice first in drawing?

Line control first: straight lines, circles and ellipses. Then boxes, cylinders and spheres in perspective, which are the building blocks of every character.

How long should I practice drawing every day?

10 to 20 minutes of focused practice is enough to progress, as long as you do it every day. A short daily session beats a long one once a week.

What are good drawing warm-up exercises?

One or two minutes of straight lines, circles and ellipses drawn in one stroke. They loosen your hand and make the rest of the session cleaner.

Do drawing exercises help with anime drawing?

Yes. Anime characters are built on the same volumes and proportions as any figure, so boxes, mannequins, gestures and simplified heads make anime drawings look more solid.

Should I use a ruler for straight line exercises?

No. The goal is to train your hand to draw straight lines freehand, so draw each line in one quick stroke without a ruler.
